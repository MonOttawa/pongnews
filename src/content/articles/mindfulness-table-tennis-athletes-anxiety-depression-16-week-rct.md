# Mindfulness Training Cuts Anxiety and Depression in Table Tennis Athletes, 16-Week RCT Finds

Table tennis athletes who practiced mindfulness meditation for just eight minutes, four times per week, showed significant improvements in cognitive performance, anxiety, and depression over a 16-week period. That is the finding of a randomized controlled trial published in the January-February 2026 issue of the American Journal of Men's Health.

The study, conducted by Qian and colleagues, enrolled 71 college table tennis athletes from Chinese universities. The experimental group completed 8-minute mindfulness sessions at home using audio recordings, consisting of mindfulness breathing and body-scan meditation. The control group received no intervention. Both groups continued their regular table tennis training throughout the study.

## The Cognitive Benefits

Athletes in the mindfulness group showed superior performance on all cognitive tests compared to the control group. The researchers measured attention, working memory, and perceptual abilities using a battery of standardized tests. These are precisely the cognitive domains that table tennis relies on during competition — tracking the ball, anticipating spin, deciding between forehand and backhand, and executing precise motor responses.

The mindfulness intervention did not require specialized equipment or facilities. Athletes simply followed guided audio recordings at home after their regular training sessions. This practicality makes the intervention scalable to athletic programs with limited budgets.

## The Mental Health Impact

Beyond cognitive performance, the mindfulness group showed meaningful improvements in mental health outcomes:

- **Reduced anxiety symptoms**
- **Reduced depression symptoms**
- **Increased subjective well-being**
- **Reduced subjective fatigue**

These improvements were measured using validated psychological questionnaires administered at baseline, 8 weeks, and 16 weeks. The fact that benefits emerged by the 8-week midpoint suggests that mindfulness can produce measurable mental health effects in a relatively short timeframe.

## The Neurophysiological Evidence

The study also measured cerebral oxygenation using functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS) while athletes performed cognitive tasks. This noninvasive technique measures blood flow and oxygen use in the brain, providing a window into neural activity.

The mindfulness group showed significantly increased oxygenated hemoglobin (HbO) levels in targeted cerebral regions during cognitive testing. This finding indicates that the intervention enhanced brain activation in areas associated with attention, executive control, and working memory — the same networks engaged during table tennis play.

The fNIRS data provides objective neurophysiological evidence that mindfulness changed how the athletes' brains were processing information during cognitive challenges. This is not merely a subjective feeling of improved focus. It is a measurable change in brain oxygenation patterns.

## Why Mindfulness Works for Table Tennis

Table tennis is an exceptionally cognitively demanding sport. The ball travels at speeds up to 70 miles per hour, leaving players with less than half a second to decide on a response. They must track a spinning ball, anticipate its trajectory, choose between multiple stroke options, and execute a precise motor movement — all while managing the psychological pressure of competition.

This cognitive load makes table tennis athletes particularly vulnerable to stress, anxiety, and performance-related mental health challenges. The American College of Sports Medicine reports that approximately 35% of elite athletes experience disordered eating, burnout, depression, or anxiety.

Mindfulness addresses these challenges by training athletes to maintain present-moment awareness without judgment. The breathing and body-scan components of the intervention teach athletes to recognize anxious thoughts, acknowledge them, and return attention to the present moment rather than spiraling into worry.

This skill translates directly to competition. When a table tennis athlete misses a crucial point, mindfulness helps them acknowledge the error, regulate their emotional response, and refocus on the next point. This emotional regulation is what separates resilient athletes from those who crumble under pressure.

## Consistent With Prior Research

The Qian study builds on a growing body of evidence linking mindfulness to improved sports performance and mental health. A 2022 study by Tebourski and colleagues published in the International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health found that mindfulness training improved performance in both basketball and table tennis players during ecological sport contexts — meaning real games, not laboratory simulations.

Earlier work by Aaghababa and colleagues in 2018 showed that mindfulness meditation enhanced flow state and performance in table tennis players. Flow, a psychological state of deep absorption in an activity, is associated with peak performance in many sports.

The convergence of evidence suggests that mindfulness is not a fringe technique but a scientifically validated mental training tool for athletes. The American Journal of Men's Health study adds to this literature by providing randomized controlled trial evidence, fNIRS neurophysiological data, and specific focus on table tennis.

## Practical Implications for Athletic Programs

The findings support several concrete recommendations for table tennis programs at the college, university, and elite levels:

- **Mindfulness should be integrated into training schedules**. The 8-minute duration and 4-times-per-week frequency used in the study represent a manageable time commitment that does not interfere with physical training.

- **Athletes should use structured guided audio recordings**. The study used standardized audio programs, which likely contributed to consistency and adherence. Coaches should provide access to professionally produced mindfulness resources rather than expecting athletes to self-direct.

- **Mental health monitoring should be routine**. The fact that college athletes showed measurable improvements in anxiety and depression after 16 weeks suggests that baseline mental health screening and periodic reassessment should be standard practice.

- **Mindfulness should begin early in athletic careers**. The study focused on college athletes, but the skills learned through mindfulness practice are valuable at any level. Introducing mindfulness training to younger athletes may prevent mental health challenges before they emerge.

## Limitations and Future Directions

The Qian study had several limitations. It was conducted entirely in Chinese universities, and cultural factors may influence how athletes respond to mindfulness interventions. The study also did not measure actual table tennis performance during competition, only cognitive test performance and mental health outcomes. Future research should examine whether the cognitive and mental health benefits translate to improved match results.

Additionally, the study did not include an active control group that received a different type of psychological intervention. This makes it difficult to determine whether the benefits are specific to mindfulness or would occur with any structured psychological training.

Despite these limitations, the study represents an important step forward. It provides rigorous randomized controlled evidence that mindfulness training can improve cognitive performance and mental health in table tennis athletes, with objective neurophysiological confirmation of brain changes.

## The Bottom Line

College table tennis athletes who practiced mindfulness meditation for eight minutes, four times per week, showed significant improvements in cognitive performance, anxiety, depression, and subjective well-being after 16 weeks. fNIRS measurements confirmed increased cerebral oxygenation during cognitive tasks, providing objective evidence of brain changes.

For athletes and coaches looking for evidence-based mental training tools, mindfulness now has solid scientific backing. It requires minimal time, no specialized equipment, and delivers measurable cognitive and mental health benefits. As pressure on athletes to perform increases, mindfulness may become as essential to training as physical conditioning and technical drills.

The message for table tennis athletes is clear: eight minutes of mindfulness per day can strengthen your brain and calm your mind. That is an investment worth making.
